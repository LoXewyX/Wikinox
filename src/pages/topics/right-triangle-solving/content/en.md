# Solving right triangles

A **right triangle** is a triangle that has one angle measuring $90^\circ$.

To solve a right triangle, we can use the **Pythagorean theorem** and the trigonometric ratios sine, cosine, and tangent.

## Elements of a right triangle

A right triangle has:

- **Hypotenuse**: the side opposite the right angle and always the longest side.
- **Opposite side**: the side opposite the angle we are using.
- **Adjacent side**: the leg that forms the angle we are using.

## Pythagorean theorem

If the legs are $a$ and $b$ and the hypotenuse is $c$:

$$
a^2+b^2=c^2
$$

For example, if we know both legs:

$$
a=3,\qquad b=4
$$

we can calculate the hypotenuse:

$$
c=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}
$$

$$
c=\sqrt{25}=5
$$

## Trigonometric ratios

:::right-triangle-diagram{mode="trigonometry"}
:::

For an acute angle $\theta$:

$$
\sin(\theta)=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
$$

$$
\cos(\theta)=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}
$$

$$
\tan(\theta)=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}
$$

A simple way to remember them is **SOH-CAH-TOA**:

- **SOH**: Sine = Opposite / Hypotenuse
- **CAH**: Cosine = Adjacent / Hypotenuse
- **TOA**: Tangent = Opposite / Adjacent

## Finding a side

If we know an angle and one side, we can calculate another side using a trigonometric ratio.

For example, if:

$$
\theta=30^\circ
$$

and the hypotenuse is $10$:

$$
\sin(30^\circ)=\frac{a}{10}
$$

Therefore:

$$
a=10\sin(30^\circ)
$$

$$
a=5
$$

## Finding an angle

If we know two sides, we can calculate an angle using the inverse trigonometric functions:

$$
\theta=\arcsin\left(\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\right)
$$

$$
\theta=\arccos\left(\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\right)
$$

$$
\theta=\arctan\left(\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\right)
$$

## Strategy for solving a triangle

1. Identify the hypotenuse.
2. Choose the angle you know or want to calculate.
3. Identify the opposite and adjacent sides.
4. Check whether you can use the Pythagorean theorem.
5. If you know an angle, use sine, cosine, or tangent.
6. If you need to find an angle, use $\arcsin$, $\arccos$, or $\arctan$.
7. Check that the results are consistent.

## Complete example

Suppose we have a right triangle with:

$$
a=6,\qquad b=8
$$

First, calculate the hypotenuse:

$$
c=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}
$$

$$
c=\sqrt{36+64}
$$

$$
c=10
$$

Now we can calculate one of the angles:

$$
\theta=\arcsin\left(\frac{6}{10}\right)
$$

Therefore:

$$
\theta\approx36.87^\circ
$$

The other acute angle is:

$$
90^\circ-36.87^\circ\approx53.13^\circ
$$

We now know all the sides and angles of the triangle.
